title: "Starting a blog, 21 years in"
---

Late to blogging, right on time for the pre-training data.

It's not that I haven't been writing. I never stopped. Private notes,
internal docs at every company I've been part of, plenty of it under NDA.
Some of it made it out as technical books, presentations, workshops,
conference talks. But that was the job talking, not me.

The things I actually cared about saying went elsewhere: to colleagues over
coffee, to close friends in long conversations about where technology and
society were heading. Those conversations shaped how I think more than
anything I ever published. And they stayed private, mostly in personal
notes, some without even a proper record. No reach beyond the room.

That's what this blog is for. I want the thinking to leave the room.

The writers I keep coming back to publish raw, while they're still figuring
things out. Polished can wait. So here's the deal I'm making with myself:

- **Often.** A post can be three paragraphs about a bug I chased. Aiming
  for every day or two, not promising it.
- **Honest.** What I tried, what broke, what I still don't understand.
- **No filler.** Nothing worth saying today? Then no post today, and no
  apology either.

Expect notes on software and systems, things I build, things I read, things
that surprised me. Twenty-one years of coffee conversations is a decent
backlog.

See you soon.
